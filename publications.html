When Should We Orchestrate Multiple Agents?

Submitted to ICML 2025 (under review)

Authors: Umang Bhatt, Sanyam Kapoor, Katherine M. Collins, Ilia Sucholutsky, Mihir Upadhyay, Francesco Quinzan, Adrian Weller, Andrew Gordon Wilson, Muhammad Bilal Zafar.

Abstract: Strategies for orchestrating interactions between multiple agents often overestimate performance and underestimate orchestration costs. We propose a framework for orchestration under realistic conditions, such as inference costs and availability constraints. Our findings show that orchestration is effective only when there are performance or cost differentials between agents. Empirical results demonstrate its utility in agent selection in simulated environments, learning strategy selection in Rogers' Paradox, and task outsourcing in human-subject question-answer experiments.
